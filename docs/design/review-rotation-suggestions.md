# Review rotation suggestions after running

The [historical replay](run-to-strength-replay.md) found where the existing rule made a decision and where it
had insufficient context. Use prospective review to check whether an adjustment fits the athlete's actual
training and whether an optional question is worth asking.

1. In Today, open the explanation and turn on **Review rotation suggestions**. Collection starts after
   consent. Today tracks visible swap, postponement and lighter-effort suggestions and the warm-up question.
   The device keeps the latest 30 entries, one per date and suggestion type.
2. Train as usual. After the next strength session or the planned training window, return to the review.
   Mark whether the suggestion helped and how the session went. Leave any uncertain response blank. A
   missing outcome is unknown. Mark **Did not train** only when you know the session did not happen.
3. Review cases voluntarily shared by the athlete with a running coach. Count suggestions shown, accepted,
   declined and unrated. Count unnecessary ratings separately. Discuss accepted changes that still needed
   adjustment and suggestions declined before an unexpectedly difficult session. Ask whether the suggested
   change fit the session's leg demand, priority and recent running. For warm-up questions, count answered,
   skipped and unnecessary ratings separately. An unanswered question gives no information about how the
   athlete's legs felt.
4. Refer decisions involving localized or worsening pain, movement limitation, illness or other warning
   symptoms to a sports clinician for review of the wording and safeguards. Obtain the coach and clinical
   review before automating these decisions. Track that work in [#319](https://github.com/droidsam1/overreach/issues/319).

Feedback includes categorical ratings, the date, the kind of suggestion and any recorded decision. It stays
on the device, outside Overreach backups and reports. Turning off review clears the entries. Share only the
cases the athlete chooses, with identifying details and any unrelated health information removed. Review
results can identify changes worth testing. They do not estimate injury risk or establish that an adjustment
improved recovery.
